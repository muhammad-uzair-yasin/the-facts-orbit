[am_adam:host] He did. His name was Hachiko.

[am_adam:host] Tokyo, 1924. A professor named Hidesaburo Ueno adopted a fluffy Akita puppy. Every evening, Hachiko waited for him at Shibuya Station.

[af_sky:asker] Aww. Every single day?

[am_adam:host] Every day. Until May 1925. The professor collapsed at work... and never came home.

[af_sky:asker] Oh no... so what did Hachiko do?

[am_adam:host] The next evening, he went to the station. And the next. And the next. For nine years and nine months... he showed up right when the train arrived.

[af_sky:asker] Nine years?!

[am_adam:host] At first, people shooed him away. Then a newspaper told his story... and all of Japan fell in love with him. In 1934, they built him a bronze statue. And Hachiko was there to see it.

[af_sky:asker] Did he ever stop waiting?

[am_adam:host] Only in March 1935, when he passed away. Today, part of his remains rest beside the professor's grave. Together again.
