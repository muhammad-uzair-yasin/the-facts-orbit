# Would you leave a room filling with smoke?

Status: approved script, awaiting Uzair's recording.
Cover word: **SMOKE**

## Script (record exactly)

Would you leave a room filling with smoke? Most people... don't.

In 1968, psychologists Latané and Darley sat students in a room to fill out a questionnaire. Then smoke started pouring in through a vent.

When students were alone, seventy-five percent got up and reported it.

Then they put a student with two strangers — who were secretly actors. The actors just... kept writing. Ignoring the smoke completely.

Now only ten percent reported it. The rest sat there, coughing, waving smoke away from their faces... and finished the form.

Why? When we're unsure, we look at other people. If they're calm, we decide nothing's wrong. And in a group, everyone feels a little less responsible.

It's called the bystander effect. And the cure is simple: be the first one to move. Point at one person and say — "You. Call for help."
